# Institutional pilot preparation

This is an unapproved planning template. No institution, participant or start date
is confirmed. Use `institution-template.json` for NGO, university, foundation,
government-agency or corporate-CSR configurations; keep nulls until supported by
receipts. A configuration file is not an implemented onboarding service.

Before any real deployment, the appointed pilot owner must supply evidence for:

1. Verified institutional identity, tenant membership and named accountable owner;
   minimum role permissions and invitation expiry/revocation tested across tenants.
2. Approved intervention, baseline, denominator, outcome method/version and feedback
   questions. Distinguish observations, verified completion and measured outcomes.
   Do not treat volunteer hours, inferred AI outputs or demo records as impact.
3. Consent language and withdrawal workflow, lawful data handling, retention and
   deletion procedures, secure evidence access and local safeguarding requirements.
4. Named safeguarding and data-protection contacts, confidential reporting routes,
   escalation response responsibilities, appeal process and conflict-of-interest rules.
5. Reviewer and coordinator training with synthetic exercises for fabricated needs,
   duplicate evidence, rejected uploads, privacy requests and emergency escalation.
6. Accessibility and usability checks with intended users, offline upload/retry
   recovery, supported-device tests and documented support responsibilities.
7. Deployment and rollback approval, backup/restore exercise, minimum observability,
   audit access, incident contacts and monitoring ownership. External gate receipts
   must identify their exact candidate commit; keep `ga=false` until applicable gates pass.
8. Explicit approval of real participants, dates, costs and commercial/support terms.

Prepare the acceptance record with candidate SHA, environment, test or approval ID,
actual date, accountable person and evidence location. Unexecuted checks remain
NOT_RUN; missing approvals remain UNCONFIRMED. A mock-only exercise may prove a
software behavior but cannot satisfy institutional approval or field validation.

Repository engineering can continue independently: build and test tenant controls,
onboarding, consent, safeguarding, training materials and reporting with clearly
marked synthetic data. Do not send invitations to real institutions or deploy a
pilot using this document alone.
